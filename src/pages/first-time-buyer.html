The short answers

First-time buyer questions I get every week in Camden County

By Nicholas Rodriguez Jr., licensed real estate agent in NJ (#1969622) and PA (#RS369752) with Keller Williams Realty Moorestown. Updated November 12, 2026.

Who counts as a first-time home buyer in New Jersey?

For New Jersey's state programs (run by NJHMFA, the New Jersey Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency), you count as a first-time buyer if you have not owned your main home in the past three years. So if you owned a home years ago, you may qualify again. Two groups don't have to be first-time buyers at all: qualified veterans, and buyers in an Urban Target Area who won't own another main home at closing. In Camden County, Urban Target Areas include Camden, Cherry Hill, Pennsauken, Gloucester City, Berlin, Pine Hill and Woodlynne.

How much down payment help can I get in Camden County, NJ?

Up to $10,000 through NJHMFA's down payment assistance, for the down payment and closing costs. It is a loan with no interest and no monthly payment, and it is forgiven after five years if you keep living in the home and don't sell or refinance. Income and purchase price limits apply, and you apply through a participating NJHMFA lender, not through the state directly (source: NJHMFA fact sheet, updated June 17, 2026). Programs change, so confirm the current amount with a participating lender before you count on it.

How much do I need saved to buy a $250,000 house in Camden County?

Plan on roughly $16,000 without help, or about $6,000 if you qualify for the full $10,000 of NJHMFA assistance. Here is the math on a $250,000 home with an FHA loan:

  • Down payment: 3.5% is $8,750
  • Closing costs: budget about 3%, which is $7,500 (lender fees, title, prepaid taxes and insurance)
  • Total before help: about $16,250
  • With $10,000 of NJHMFA help: about $6,250

Keep a cushion on top of that for the inspection, the appraisal and moving. Your lender's Loan Estimate gives your real closing costs within days of applying.

What does a $250,000 home really cost each month in Camden County?

About $2,450 a month, and property tax is the part most buyers miss. With 3.5% down on an FHA loan at a 6.75% rate over 30 years:

  • Principal and interest: about $1,565
  • Property tax at Camden County's roughly 3.1% average: about $646
  • Homeowners insurance at $1,500 a year: about $125
  • FHA mortgage insurance at 0.55% a year: about $111

Most online calculators assume about 1.1% property tax, which makes the same house look roughly $400 a month cheaper than it is here. Your town's actual tax rate will differ, so run your own numbers in the payment calculator before you shop.

Which loans let first-time buyers put 3% down, 3.5% down, or nothing down?

  • FHA: 3.5% down with a credit score of 580 or higher. The most common choice when credit is still being built.
  • Conventional: as little as 3% down for many first-time buyers, usually with a stronger credit score. Mortgage insurance can be removed later once you have enough equity.
  • VA: 0% down for eligible veterans and service members.
  • USDA: 0% down, but only in eligible rural areas and under income limits. Much of Camden County doesn't qualify, so check the address first.

NJHMFA's assistance can be paired with these loans through a participating lender. I'm not a lender, but I can introduce you to lenders who use New Jersey's first-time buyer programs often.

What are the steps to buying your first home in New Jersey?

  1. Check your budget and credit. Know your real monthly number, taxes included, before you look at listings.
  2. Get pre-approved by a lender, and ask whether you qualify for NJHMFA help.
  3. Sign a buyer agreement with your agent. Written buyer agreements are now required in New Jersey and Pennsylvania, and commission is agreed in writing before any work starts.
  4. Tour homes in the towns and price range that fit your number.
  5. Make an offer. Your agent writes it and negotiates price, credits and dates.
  6. Attorney review. In New Jersey, a signed contract usually goes through a three-business-day attorney review period, when either side's attorney can change or cancel it.
  7. Inspection and appraisal. I translate inspection findings into real dollars so you know what to ask for.
  8. Clear to close, final walk-through, and closing. You sign, the loan funds, and you get the keys.
